\textbf{Both the optimisation and the learning contribute.} The economic rule alone reaches 0.870 and the LP alone 0.957. The final models reach 0.991, so learning on top of the LP removes about four fifths of the LP's remaining error. The other direction holds too: without the LP features, the best LightGBM model reached 0.979 (exp 009).

\textbf{The largest gains came from describing how the plants depend on each other.} Of the development steps, the Tokke--Vinje coupling (+0.013), the first LP (+0.004) and the LP's routing fix (+0.005) gave the largest gains. Changes of the model itself, such as other predictor types, tuning (Section~\ref{sec:6}) or a blend of the two final models (+0.0001), gave little or nothing.

\textbf{The validation reflected the test period.} The public leaderboard score rose with the validation score at every submitted step, from 0.99042 (exp 017) to 0.99414 (exp 029). The locked 2018 holdout (0.9877 and 0.9876) lies within the range of the four validation years (0.9868--0.9941), so we see no sign that our many decisions overfitted those four years.

\textbf{Both final models are above the highest virtual team.} On the public leaderboard, LightGBM scores 0.99414 and XGBoost 0.99429, against 0.99040 for the highest virtual team (VT1), a margin of about 0.004. The final grade uses the private leaderboard, which we cannot see. As a check, we compared the final model with exp 017, which scored almost exactly as VT1 on the public leaderboard. The final model is better in all four validation years, by between 0.003 (2021) and 0.013 (2020), even though these years are very different. We therefore expect the margin to hold on the private part, although VT1 is a different model from ours and this cannot be guaranteed.

\textbf{Why the public score is higher than our validation mean.} A possible explanation is that 2023--2024 are easier than our hardest validation year, 2020, with its extremely low prices. The two final models differ by at most 0.0002 in the validation mean, the holdout and the public score, which is within noise. We submitted both because they are equally good but built by different libraries.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Both short notebooks run from the raw data in about 9 minutes on a laptop and reproduce our submitted files exactly.
