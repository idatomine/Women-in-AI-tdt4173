\begin{disposition}
What to say:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Both models clear the highest virtual team (0.99040) on the public leaderboard by about 0.004.
\item
  Compared with the predictors without learning (Section~\ref{sec:5.compare}), the final models raise the AUC from 0.870 (economic rule) and 0.957 (LP alone) to 0.991, so both the physics and the learning contribute.
\item
  Of the development steps, the two largest gains came from adding information about how the plants depend on each other: the Tokke--Vinje coupling (+0.013) and the LP with its routing fix (+0.004 and +0.005). The public leaderboard rose with the validation score at every submitted step (0.99042 → 0.99414), so our validation reflected the test period.
\item
  The 2018 holdout (0.9877 / 0.9876) lies within the range of the validation folds (0.9868--0.9941), so we see no sign that our choices overfitted the four folds.
\item
  The public leaderboard score is higher than our validation mean. Possible explanation: 2023--2024 may be easier than our hardest validation year, 2020, with its extremely low prices.
\item
  Caveat: the final grade uses the private leaderboard, which we cannot see; differences of ±0.001 between our two models are noise.
\item
  Reproducibility: both short notebooks run from the raw data in about 9 minutes on a laptop and reproduce our submitted files byte for byte.
\end{itemize}
\end{disposition}
